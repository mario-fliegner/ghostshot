# Core Product Identity

The application is fundamentally:

- a precision recreation camera
- based on overlay alignment
- focused on reproducing the same perspective/viewpoint
- visually driven
- emotionally activated through recreated photos
- deterministic in compare rendering
- built around exact recreation of framing and perspective

The app itself explains its value visually very quickly.

Because of that, the name does NOT need to fully explain the entire workflow.

The name only needs to:
- not create false expectations
- not sound cringe
- not create toxic associations
- survive store search
- survive social media usage
- work internationally
- feel modern and stable

---

# Brand Direction Goals

The branding intentionally moved away from:

- gimmicky startup naming
- AI-style product names
- over-technical photography terms
- film production wording
- developer-tool language
- generic “camera app” naming
- social media sounding names

The final direction aimed for:

- clean
- modern
- minimal
- trustworthy
- international
- visual
- utility-first
- emotionally neutral
- premium-feeling
- non-cringe

The app should feel like:

> a precise photographic tool with emotional results

—not like:
- a social network
- an AI toy
- a filter app
- a nostalgia slideshow
- a CAD utility
- a developer/debugging tool
- a cheap Android camera app

# Original Naming Direction

## GhostShot

Originally used as internal app/project name.

Problems identified:
- paranormal/ghost-photo association
- Snapchat/gimmick vibe
- not trustworthy enough for release
- weak long-term branding
- not ideal for Play Store perception

Conclusion:
Rejected as public product name.

---

## Then & Now Camera

Temporary descriptive working title.

Problems:
- extremely generic
- no product identity
- impossible branding
- weak memorability
- category name instead of actual product

Conclusion:
Useful descriptively, but not viable as final public name.

---

# Rejected Naming Directions

---

## “Retake” Direction

Examples:
- SceneRetake
- FrameRetake
- ReShot

Problems:
- film production association
- mistake-correction vibe
- Hollywood/movie-set feeling
- sounds like video editing software
- not aligned with emotional recreation concept

Conclusion:
Rejected.

---

## “Trace / Align / Match” Direction

Examples:
- AlignCam
- TraceView
- MatchFrame

Problems:
- CAD / engineering vibe
- developer-tool feeling
- too technical
- emotionally cold
- utility-tool associations

Conclusion:
Rejected.

---

## “Cam” Naming

Examples:
- AlignCam
- Respot Cam

Problems:
- cheap Android utility vibe
- low-quality camera-app feeling
- ad-supported utility association
- visually outdated branding style

Conclusion:
Rejected completely.

---

## “Respot”

One of the strongest alternative candidates.

Strengths:
- short
- modern
- visually memorable
- startup-like
- unique searchability

Problems identified:
- “Repost” mishearing risk
- audio confusion during word-of-mouth
- GPS/location-app associations
- wildlife/spotting associations
- weak direct photography signal

Conclusion:
Strong candidate, but long-term friction risk remained.

---

## “SameFrame”

Very important transition step.

Strengths:
- semantically close to actual app behavior
- same framing / same perspective
- technically aligned with app architecture
- deterministic recreation concept matched well
- good pronunciation
- internationally understandable

Problems:
- picture-frame / collage app associations
- “frame app” store-category drift
- slightly too technical/mechanical
- weaker emotional feeling

Conclusion:
Strong functional candidate.
Eventually evolved into “SameView”.

---

# Why “SameView” Won

The shift from:

> Frame → View

became the decisive improvement.

---

## Why “View” worked better

Compared to “Frame”, “View” felt:

- more human
- more visual
- more photographic
- more emotional
- more spatial
- less technical
- less mechanical
- less utility-like
- less associated with collage/frame apps

“View” better represents:

- same perspective
- same viewpoint
- same visual position
- recreating the same sightline
- standing where someone once stood

This matched the actual emotional core of the app significantly better.

---

# Why NOT “Then & Now”

The app is NOT primarily:
- a passive comparison viewer
- a nostalgia gallery
- a timeline slideshow

The app is ACTIVE.

The user:
- aligns
- recreates
- repositions
- captures
- reproduces perspective

Therefore “Then & Now” was intentionally NOT used as the primary branding identity.

# Store / Social / Branding Reality

The final branding strategy assumed:

The app will primarily be understood through:
- screenshots
- overlay demos
- TikTok/Reels/Shorts
- before/after recreations
- visual storytelling

NOT through the name alone.

Therefore:
a clean, stable, non-cringe name was more important than an over-explanatory name.

# Domain Strategy

## sameview.app

Chosen because:
- short
- clean
- modern
- premium-feeling
- internationally readable
- easy pronunciation
- no hyphens
- no strange spelling

The “.app” TLD also directly reinforces:
- mobile-app identity
- modern software branding
- clean product presentation

without needing additional words like:
- camera
- cam
- app
- overlay

inside the actual product name.

---

# Social Media Strategy

The social strategy intentionally separated:

- clean product name
- practical handle availability

This resulted in:

- SameView as the visible brand
- @sameviewapp as consistent social handles

This avoided:
- underscores
- numbers
- hyphenated handles
- awkward fallback naming

while maintaining consistent cross-platform branding.

- technical correctness
- emotional relevance
- international usability
- Play Store perception

- pronunciation
- visual branding
- long-term stability

- hype

- visual coherence
